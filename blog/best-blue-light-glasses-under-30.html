Sizing Cheat Sheet

  • Typical lens width range: 48-54mm
  • Typical bridge width range: 16-20mm
  • Typical temple length range: 135-145mm
  • Compare these ranges to the numbers printed on the inside of your current frames' temple arms to judge whether a new pair will likely sit narrower or wider than what you already wear.

Key takeaways

  • Non-prescription blue light glasses are typically sold ready-to-wear, so checking lens width and bridge measurements against a pair you already own is the main way to judge fit before ordering.
  • Frame styles in this category range from classic wayfarer shapes to jeweled and crystal-tone designs, so style choice is rarely limited once price is capped around $30.
  • Blue light filtering claims are set by each brand's own marketing and are not independently verified on this page, so reading the retailer's specific description matters more than the frame's price.
  • Brands such as Eydology and Dima Eyewear offer several frame colors within the same base shape, which can simplify comparing fit across options.
  • Return and exchange policies differ by retailer, so confirming the policy before ordering is worth more than comparing price alone.

How to Choose Blue Light Glasses Under $30

  1. Compare frame measurements to glasses you already own. Checking lens width, bridge width, and temple length against a current pair helps avoid ordering a frame that sits too tight or too loose, since non-prescription frames in this category are typically sold in one general size.
  2. Read the retailer's specific blue light claim. Terms like "blue light blocking" vary by brand and are not independently verified here, so understanding what each listing actually states prevents assuming a stronger benefit than described.
  3. Decide between a neutral or statement frame style. Choosing between understated black or tortoise versus a jeweled or crystal-tone frame upfront narrows the options and avoids returning a frame that doesn't match your usual style.
  4. Confirm whether the pair is non-prescription only. All the frames in this guide are non-prescription, so readers needing vision correction should check Eydology's prescription glasses line instead of assuming a lens upgrade is available.
  5. Check the retailer's return and exchange policy. Online frame fit can be hard to judge from photos alone, so knowing the exchange window before buying reduces the risk of being stuck with an ill-fitting frame.
  6. Verify current price and stock directly on the retailer page. Listings and availability can change, so confirming details at checkout avoids relying on outdated information.

Common buying mistakes

  • Assuming all blue light glasses filter the same amount of light: filtering claims differ by brand and are set by marketing, not a shared standard, so compare the specific wording each retailer uses rather than assuming equivalence.
  • Skipping frame measurements: ordering without comparing lens width and temple length to an existing pair can result in a frame that pinches or slides, so check the sizing cheat sheet before buying.
  • Choosing a decorative frame for daily office wear: jeweled or heavily embellished styles may not suit every workplace setting, so matching frame style to where it will be worn avoids a mismatch.
  • Expecting prescription lenses in a non-prescription listing: all frames covered here are non-prescription, so readers needing corrective lenses should check a dedicated prescription glasses page instead.
  • Ignoring the return policy: online eyewear can look different in person, and not knowing the exchange terms beforehand can leave a buyer without recourse if the fit is off.
  • Buying based on color alone: frame shape affects fit as much as color does, so consider both before deciding, especially when a style is only available in a few shades.
  • Overlooking that details may change: specifications, colors, and availability listed by any retailer can be updated, so always confirm current information on the official product page before completing a purchase.

Frequently asked questions

Do blue light glasses under $30 work as well as pricier options?

This depends on the specific coating and brand claims, which are not standardized across price points. Lower-priced frames can carry the same general blue-light-filtering marketing as pricier ones, but filtering strength and lens quality are set by each manufacturer rather than price alone, so reading the retailer's description matters more than cost.

Can I get blue light glasses with a prescription in this price range?

The frames featured in this guide are non-prescription only. Readers who need corrective lenses should check a dedicated prescription glasses page, such as Eydology's prescription line, rather than expecting a non-prescription blue light listing to include lens correction options.

How do I know if a frame will fit my face?

Compare the lens width, bridge width, and temple length listed by the retailer to the same measurements on glasses you already wear. If a listing doesn't provide exact dimensions, as is common among the frames covered here, use the general ranges in the sizing cheat sheet as a rough guide.

Are tortoise and crystal frame finishes just cosmetic differences?

Mostly yes, since finish and color typically affect appearance rather than the blue-light-filtering function of the lens. Choosing between tortoise, crystal, or solid tones is a style decision, while any filtering claims should be checked separately in the product description.

Can I return blue light glasses if they don't fit?

This depends entirely on the individual retailer's policy, which is not detailed in the product information supplied here. Check the return and exchange terms on the Eydology, Abracadabra NYC, or Dima Eyewear site before ordering so you know your options if the fit isn't right.

Is a wayfarer shape better than a rounder frame for blocking blue light?

Frame shape does not determine how much blue light a lens filters; that depends on the lens coating itself, not the outline of the frame. Choose a shape like the Bolted Wayfar based on style and fit preference rather than assuming it offers different filtering than a rounder style.

Why do some listings include jewels or decorative accents?

These are style choices offered by certain retailers, such as the jeweled frame from Abracadabra NYC, aimed at readers who want a more decorative look. They don't necessarily indicate a different level of blue-light-filtering performance compared with plainer frames.

Should I buy multiple colors of the same frame style?

This is a personal preference rather than a fit or performance consideration, since colorways like Gage Black and Gage Tortoise share the same non-prescription build. Buying more than one color can make sense if you want variety for different settings, as long as the shape already fits well.
