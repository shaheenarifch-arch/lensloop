Best Glasses for an Oval Face Shape

By Shaheen Arif, Founder and Editor · Reviewed by Talha N. Ch · Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

If you have an oval face, congratulations: you have the most versatile face shape for eyewear. The balanced proportions of an oval face mean most frame styles will look good on you. But "most styles work" does not mean "all styles are equal." Some frames will enhance your features, while others can throw off your natural balance. Here is how to find the best glasses for your oval face.

Understanding the Oval Face Shape

An oval face is characterized by:

  • A face that is slightly longer than it is wide (roughly a 1.5:1 ratio)
  • Cheekbones that are the widest part of the face
  • A forehead that is slightly wider than the jawline
  • A gently rounded chin without sharp angles

The key to choosing glasses for an oval face is maintaining this natural balance. Frames should be as wide as (or slightly wider than) the broadest part of your face. Avoid anything that disrupts the proportional harmony you naturally have.

Best Frame Styles for Oval Faces

Square and Rectangular Frames

Angular frames add structure and contrast to the soft curves of an oval face. They create a strong, defined look that works well in professional settings. A medium-width rectangular frame is perhaps the single most flattering shape for oval faces. Look for frames with clean corners and moderate thickness.

Browline (Clubmaster) Frames

The classic browline shape draws attention to the upper half of the face, emphasizing your cheekbones and brow. This style works beautifully on oval faces because it adds visual weight where the face is already strongest. Both vintage-inspired tortoise and modern metal browlines work well.

Aviator Frames

The teardrop shape of aviators complements oval faces without overwhelming them. Full-metal aviators offer a classic look, while modern variations with acetate bridges add a contemporary twist. Just make sure the frame is not so large that it extends past your cheekbones.

Round Frames

This might seem counterintuitive since oval is already a rounded shape, but round frames can create a striking, fashion-forward look on oval faces. The key is proportion: choose round frames that are slightly smaller than your face width for a vintage intellectual vibe, or go bold with oversized rounds for a statement look.

Cat-Eye Frames

Cat-eye frames with their upswept corners add lift and drama. They draw attention upward and outward, which is flattering on oval faces. Subtle cat-eyes work for everyday wear, while dramatic winged versions are perfect for making a statement.

Frames to Approach with Caution

While oval faces have the most flexibility, a few styles can be tricky:

  • Very narrow frames: Frames that are significantly narrower than your face can make an oval face appear longer and thinner than it is.
  • Oversized frames that are too tall: Frames that cover too much of your face can hide your balanced proportions. Go wide but not excessively tall.
  • Frames with heavy bottom rims: These drag the eye downward and can make the lower face appear heavier. If you like bottom-heavy frames, choose thin ones.

How to Choose the Right Size

Frame size matters as much as frame shape. Here is a quick sizing guide:

  • Frame width: Should match or be slightly wider than the widest part of your face (your cheekbones). Measure from temple to temple.
  • Lens width: Typically 48-54mm for medium faces. Your eyes should appear centered in each lens, not pushed to one side.
  • Bridge width: 16-20mm for most noses. The bridge should sit comfortably without pinching or sliding.
  • Temple length: 135-145mm is standard. Temples should reach your ears without bending sharply.

Most online retailers, including Eydology, list these measurements for every frame. Browse frames with detailed sizing. For more help choosing, visit our eyeglasses guide.

Color and Material Tips

Oval faces can carry both bold and subtle frame colors. Consider your skin tone and hair color:

  • Warm skin tones: Tortoise, gold, warm brown, olive, and honey-colored frames.
  • Cool skin tones: Black, silver, blue, gray, and clear frames.
  • Neutral skin tones: You can go either way. Classic tortoise and black work universally.

Frequently Asked Questions

What glasses look best on an oval face?
Oval faces suit most frame shapes. Square and rectangular frames add definition, while oversized rounds make a style statement. Avoid frames that are too narrow or too wide for your face.
Should oval faces avoid any frame shapes?
Oval faces are the most versatile for eyewear. The main pitfall is frames that are disproportionately small or narrow, which can make the face appear longer.
How do I know if my face is oval?
An oval face is slightly longer than it is wide, with a gently rounded jawline and forehead. The widest point is at the cheekbones. The chin tapers softly without being pointed.
